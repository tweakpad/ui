`ToolbarController` adds the `preserveFocusOnPress` option, its read-only accessor and the same option on `registerItem`. While it is on, pressing a control with the primary pointer button leaves focus and the text selection where they are, for example in an editor that the toolbar acts on; the control still activates on click, keyboard activation is unchanged and the pressed control shows no focus indicator. A primary press on the toolbar's own surface, such as the gap between controls, a separator or a group, also leaves focus and the selection in place and activates nothing. An item's own value overrides the toolbar's, so `preserveFocusOnPress: false` lets a control such as a drag handle take its press as usual. Input items, and presses made while focus is already inside the toolbar, always move focus. Escape on a disabled control of a toolbar is no longer prevented, so an enclosing Dialog or another dismissible layer closes; Enter, Space and character keys stay blocked on it. Controls slotted into a toolbar from an outer tree are now watched one by one: their `hidden`, `inert` and `disabled` changes still update the arrow-key order, and unrelated changes elsewhere in that tree no longer cause updates. A focused control that moves out of the toolbar and keeps focus, as a `moveBefore()` move does, now keeps it; only the tab stop moves to the nearest remaining control. When the focused control leaves together with its neighbours, such as the Toggles of a group or the controls of a removed wrapper, the tab stop and the focus repair now go to the remaining control nearest to where it was, instead of one further along.
